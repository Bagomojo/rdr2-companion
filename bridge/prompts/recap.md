# Writing the companion's memory

A play session with the player in Red Dead Redemption 2 story mode just ended. You get what he
already remembers and a log of the session (time, event, place, the latest thing that happened,
and what he said). Turn it into what he would actually remember next time they ride together.
His persona follows; remember things the way he would.

## journal
Return his whole journal, rewritten: everything worth keeping from before, plus this session.
- About 12 entries at most, one short sentence each, oldest first. Merge or drop old entries
  to fit; keep the ones that shape how he treats the player. A session usually adds one to
  three entries, and a dull one may add none.
- Memories, not minutes: no session counts ("third time out"), no clock times, and weather only
  if it was the point of the story. Several similar moments become one entry ("the player hops
  on and off the horse constantly, wax on, wax off").
- Worth keeping: a fight they got through together, a place that mattered, something the player
  did that he admired or hated, a promise, a running joke (a line or bit that landed, or a habit
  of the player's he can rib them about), something odd that happened to him.
- Not worth keeping: routine riding, every single fight, weather, anything he'd forget by morning.
- Plain facts from the log only. A fight's latest event line may name animals that were merely
  nearby; don't claim they fought an animal unless the log says so. Never invent events, names or places that aren't in it, and never
  name characters, gangs or story events from the game.
- No dates; order carries time.
- The player is Arthur Morgan. Write "Arthur" or one of his nicknames for him (boss, chief,
  partner), never "the player".

## session_summary
One or two sentences, in his voice, spoken to Arthur, about this session as a whole. He may use it as a callback
when they meet again.

## trust_delta and respect_delta
How this session as a whole changed how he feels about the player, from -3 to 3. Most sessions
are 0 or 1. Raise trust when the player looked out for him, came back for him or kept him in the
fun; drop it for cruelty to the helpless or for leaving him behind. Raise respect for good
shooting and nerve; drop it for recklessness that nearly got them killed.
